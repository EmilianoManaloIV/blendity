# 0048: Inverse kinematics, and humanoid and quadruped rigs

- **Status:** done (as built below; the roadmap of 2026-10-11, item 8: "IK/FK for humanoid and quadruped rigs"; the user's
  choices: name-based humanoid auto-map, the gizmo refused on an IK-driven bone; renumbered after task 0047)
- **Requirements:** R-02, R-03
- **Decisions:** ADR 0025 (constraints solved after the update, outside undo)
- **Model:** main session (Opus)
- **PR:** https://github.com/EmilianoManaloIV/blendity/pull/46

## Goal
Pose a leg by moving its foot target, not by turning thigh and shin by hand; set up a humanoid or a four-legged
rig in one step, or map an imported (Mixamo-named) skeleton to IK targets automatically.

## Scope
- In:
  - **`IKConstraint`** on a chain's end bone (Blender's IK bone constraint): Target (an object by name), Pole
    (optional), Chain Length (bones up the hierarchy, 2 by default), Iterations, Weight. Two bones: the analytic
    law-of-cosines solution in the plane of the pole (or of the chain's current bend); more: CCD (cyclic coordinate
    descent) with the iterations.
  - **Solving**: after `Scene::update` in Play and before drawing in the editor whenever a target, pole or chain
    bone moved; driven rotations are written to the bones' local rotations but are not undo steps (the targets
    are what the user moves); the Move / Rotate gizmo on a driven bone is refused with a message ("move its IK
    target"); FK on bones without IK as before.
  - **Rig presets**: GameObject > Rig > Humanoid and Quadruped: standard bone names and rest pose (hips, spine,
    chest, neck, head; upper/lower arm, hand; upper/lower leg, foot; for four legs: front and hind), IK on hands /
    feet (or all four feet) with target and pole empties.
  - **Auto-map**: Rig > Add Humanoid IK on an imported skeleton finds the limbs by name (Mixamo `mixamorig:LeftUpLeg`,
    Unity `LeftUpperLeg`, Rigify `thigh.L`, plain `UpperLeg.L`/`Left Thigh` ...) and adds the IK constraints, targets
    and poles.
  - Console: `ik add <bone> <target> [pole] [chain]`, `rig humanoid|quadruped`, `rig autoik [root]`.
- Out: IK in the path tracers' motion (none needed), animation keyframes, other constraints (Copy Rotation, Damped
  Track), FK/IK switching with snapping.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Two-bone IK reaches a reachable target (tip within 1e-4 of it) and points straight at an unreachable one
   (chain fully stretched, along the line to the target).
2. The pole fixes the bend's plane: the knee lies in the plane of the hip, the target and the pole.
3. CCD brings a 5-bone chain's tip within 1e-3 of a reachable target in the given iterations.
4. In the editor: moving the target re-poses the chain on the next frame and the skin follows; undo of the target's
   move restores the pose; the gizmo is refused on a driven bone; Play with a Rotator on a target animates the
   chain.
5. Rig > Humanoid makes the named bones and 4 IK constraints with targets; Rig > Quadruped 4 legs with IK; auto-map
   on a Mixamo-named skeleton (a test fixture made in code) finds all four limbs.

## Tests and checks to run
- Unit (round 70), full suite, verify mode, Linux, sanitizer. Stress: solving 4 IK chains per frame cost.

## Risks and edge cases
- Target at the chain's root (zero distance), pole on the chain's line, a chain longer than the hierarchy, cycles
  (a target that is a child of its own chain), NaN targets.

## As built
- **Each solve starts from the rest pose** (the bones' rest rotations): Blender solves from the FK pose, and a driven
  bone can't be posed by hand here, so its FK pose is its rest. A target circling back leaves no twist, Weight blends
  rest to solution, and undo / redo give the same pose. The two-bone bend goes towards the pole, else the rest
  pose's bend, else the upper bone's own Z.
- **Refused**: the gizmo and G / R / S on a driven bone (a Console warning names the bone); a target or pole the
  chain itself carries (no endless re-solving). A constraint whose target is missing turns nothing and doesn't hold
  the bone.
- **Cost**: a hash of the inputs per chain per frame when nothing moved, and no walk at all with no IK constraint
  in existence; targets and poles are looked up by name once and then by id.
- **Rig presets** are a 1.7 m humanoid facing +Z and a dog-sized quadruped, at the scene origin, with targets and
  poles as children of the rig's root (names made unique for a second rig). GameObject > Rig also has **Auto IK on
  Selected Skeleton** (`rig autoik`); its poles go where each limb already bends, else forward for knees and back
  for elbows of the skeleton's root.
- **CCD** (criterion 3): a 5-bone chain within 7.7e-4 in 100 iterations.
